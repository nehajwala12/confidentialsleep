Plan section 3

Sleep Media earns the money that Sleep Care spends.

OneCare Media, LLC trades as Sleep Doctor and runs two businesses under one borrower. Sleep Media publishes sleepfoundation.org, sleepapnea.org and the editorial pages of sleepdoctor.com, and earns affiliate commissions and advertising. Sleep Care sells home sleep apnea tests, CPAP equipment and resupply, for cash and since 2026 through insurance. Sleep Media's profit funds Sleep Care's loss and the shared overhead, and the company as a whole loses money.

What holds. Sleep Media earns a profit in 2027 on every case in the model, including the Severe one. Its own team is eight people, so the saving in a stand-alone company comes from removing Sleep Care and the shared layer and not from cutting the people who earn the revenue.

What to watch. The 2026 forecast. It needs Sleep Media's revenue in September to December to be 41% above the same months of 2025, after eight months that ran 18% below. The trailing figure is the safer base for a buyer and for this plan.

Sleep Media standing alone. A stand-alone Sleep Media needs about $2.6M of corporate cost on the earlier bottom-up estimate. On the 2026 forecast that leaves $10.5M to $10.7M of EBITDA. On the twelve months to August 2026, which are all actual, it leaves about $8.7M.

Where Sleep Media's revenue comes from. One site and one category carry it.

  • sleepfoundation.org earns about $13.7M of the $16.3M forecast, or 84%. The editorial pages on sleepdoctor.com earn $1.3M, advertising and other lines $1.1M, and other domains $0.2M.
  • About 70% of sleepfoundation.org's revenue comes from its best-mattress pages. A click on those pages earns about $16 against under $2 for pillows.
  • The largest affiliate partner, 3Z, guarantees $16 to $20 a click on 15 pages until 31 December 2026. That floor is worth about $0.2M a month, and no 2027 terms have been seen.
  • Non-search lines are small: advertising about $1.1M in the 2026 forecast, YouTube about $0.6M, email about $0.25M from 3.26M subscribers.

Sleep Care. Sleep Care's loss has roughly doubled during 2026 while its revenue has been flat.

  • For 2026 the plan shows revenue of $20.3M, cost of goods of $10.7M, payroll of $7.2M, marketing of $8.8M, other cost of $3.4M and EBITDA of −$9.8M.
  • For 2027 management plans revenue of $55.5M and EBITDA of −$3.7M, with break-even moved from November 2026 to October 2027. Management's own base case needs a $10M revolver from April 2027 (section 4).
  • Recurring profit, which is what a buyer of the patient book prices, was $0.66M in January to August, or about $1.0M a year, against a plan of $1.5M for 2026.
  • November is the best acquisition evidence of the year (section 8). It improves the case for a sale now. It does not supply the cash the plan needs.

Plan section 6

A March sale beats a hold unless bids come in low.

The steps for the next ten weeks are the same whether Sleep Media is later held or sold: decline, forbear, ring-fence, cap Sleep Care, take control. The choice between holding and selling can therefore wait for evidence, and the evidence arrives by mid-January. The plan runs both tracks until then and sets the rule for choosing in advance.

What holds. On each case a sale closing in March is worth more to Comvest than holding to mid-2028 at the same multiple, by $1.3M to $1.9M. A sale also ends the exposure to the Severe case, to the December 2027 maturity and to the cost of ownership.

What to watch. The multiple buyers offer while the core update is open and the 2027 partner terms are unsigned. If March bids come in a quarter to half a turn below what a buyer would pay for a company with a clean stand-alone record, holding is worth more.

The decision rule

The model values a March sale at a multiple of forward EBITDA, plus cash on hand, less selling cost, transition cost and a reserve. It values a hold as the cash swept to mid-2028 plus a sale then. Setting the two equal gives the multiple at which Comvest is indifferent.

The rule, in four steps.

  1. Name the case from the December evidence: the click level after the update and the 2027 partner terms actually signed.
  2. Sell if the best net bid is at or above the point of indifference for that case. That is about $30.4M of enterprise value on the High case, $23.5M on the Central case and $17.4M on the Low case, or 3.0 to 3.2 times forward EBITDA.
  3. Hold if the best bid is below about two and a half times forward EBITDA, and the evidence does not point to the Severe case. Run Sleep Media as a stand-alone company and return to market after two clean quarters, with a review on 30 September 2027.
  4. In between, prefer the sale, or close the gap with an earn-out on 2027 search revenue so that the lenders keep part of the High case. At equal value a lender should take the certain cash.

Listed publishers that depend on search trade at about three times EBITDA, and brokered content sites change hands at 2.0 to 2.8 times annual profit. The market multiple and the indifference multiple are close, which is why real bids should decide.

The smaller team does not move the rule. The operating plan sets out a core of six people for MediaCo from early 2028. On a hold it would add about $1.1M to Comvest's value if a buyer in mid-2028 pays for the whole saving, about $0.5M if it pays for half, and nothing if it adds the cost back. At full credit the point of indifference would rise to about 3.4 to 3.5 times. The rule stays on the $5.2M budget, because the saving cannot be proved before the gate and because any buyer can make it too.

In the Severe case a run-off is the alternative to a low bid. A run-off with one or two people is worth about $3.4M to $7.3M to the pool, depending on how fast traffic then falls (run-off). A serious bid is therefore one above about $3M to $4M of enterprise value.

Plan section 6

What a live sale track means

  • From 1 December, a sell-side adviser acceptable to the lenders approaches a short list of buyers for Sleep Media in confidence, and asks for written, non-binding indications by mid-January. Each indication states scope, liabilities assumed and closing conditions (section 11).

  • The Sleep Care track opens on the same day (section 8).

Weekly clicks on sleepfoundation.org below 176K start a sale review at once. The trigger is read with collected revenue per click and not on traffic alone. At 197K the margin is 12%.

Plan section 7

Three tools reach control, and consent is the cheapest.

Vistria's equity is out of the money and it has said it will add no cash. Its remaining economic interest is the 26.8% of the senior loan held by Vistria Credit, which shares in every dollar the lenders recover. A handover with a release therefore serves both sides. If Vistria delays, the loan documents are expected to give the lenders two further tools that do not need its signature.

What holds. Out-of-court handovers are now the most common outcome when a private-credit borrower cannot support its debt and the sponsor will not fund it. The usual trade is the keys for a mutual release.

What to watch. Authority. Comvest's 58.9% is an economic share from April 2024. Whether it can direct each step alone, and which steps need every lender or Vistria Credit, is decided by documents that have not been seen.

The tools

The second tool matters for this company. A board change moves control without moving ownership, so it does not touch Sleep Care's Medicare enrolment and does not make the lenders the owner of a healthcare business. It is cheaper and faster than a sale of collateral, and it can be followed by one.

Plan section 7

A forbearance protects Sleep Media's cash while the deal is signed.

Comvest declines the request and offers a forbearance to 29 January 2027. A forbearance is the lenders agreeing not to enforce, which the required lenders can usually give. It is not an amendment to the interest terms, which may need every affected lender. Counsel confirms which applies here.

Plan section 7

Either structure ends with a lender-owned MediaCo.

Two structures reach the same end state, a lender-owned MediaCo. They differ in what else the lenders end up owning.

The working structure, in five steps.

  1. Now. Forbearance and ring-fence. Nothing here changes ownership or is visible to Google.
  2. At the handover, February to March. Sleep Media's assets pass to MediaCo. The lenders either accept them in partial satisfaction of the debt with the borrower's consent, or bid debt at a public sale of collateral. Counsel chooses the mechanism. The amount of debt satisfied is sized to the indications from the sale track, and the lenders hold MediaCo in proportion to their loans.
  3. The debt. The senior loan falls by the amount of the credit bid. If Sleep Media is held, MediaCo carries a new loan from the lenders sized to the business, for example about three times EBITDA, maturing in 2030 with a cash sweep. That removes the December 2027 maturity and returns cash as debt service. The rest of the senior claim stays against the existing company.
  4. The existing company. It keeps Sleep Care, Falcon's notes and the legacy liabilities. An independent director runs the Sleep Care sale on a short, funded budget, and the proceeds go to the lenders under their lien.
  5. Releases and services. A mutual release with Vistria. Assignment of Sleep Media's contracts. A licence of the Sleep Doctor name back to Sleep Care. Transition services until MediaCo runs its own accounts, mail and finance.

If the sale route is chosen at the gate, step 2 becomes a sale to the buyer in place of a credit bid, and step 3 falls away.

Plan section 7

Each step needs its own consent, and each party wants something different.

The parties

Vistria Credit's strip. The strip shares pro rata by default, which costs nothing. An earlier idea was for MediaCo to take it out for about $7M, the value at the June mark. A take-out adds value for Comvest where the price is below Vistria Credit's share of what the lenders eventually recover.

The plan therefore leaves the strip sharing pro rata, offers the swap, and does not pay $7M.

Falcon. Falcon receives nothing on any case in this plan. Its lien status, standstill period, purchase option and objection rights are unknown, and they can delay a transfer. Counsel reads the subordination agreement before any notice is sent.

Protection against challenge

  • An independent decision-maker. The independent director or restructuring officer takes the borrower's decisions. The lenders' board adviser has no vote and stays out of them.
  • A market record. The sale track gives third-party evidence of value for any credit bid.
  • A release. The forbearance and the handover each carry a release of claims against the lenders, as counsel advises.
  • Related parties. Any contract between the business and a party related to a lender, a director or an adviser needs disinterested approval and arm's-length terms.
